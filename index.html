What is Diwali?

Diwali is the festival of lights. It celebrates light over darkness, good over evil and knowledge over ignorance.

Traditions

🪔

Lighting diyas

Small oil lamps glow at doors, windows and temples.

🎨

Making rangoli

Colourful patterns at the entrance welcome guests and good luck.

🙏

Lakshmi Puja

Families pray to Lakshmi and Ganesha for health, wealth and happiness.

🎁

Gifts and family

People visit relatives, exchange gifts and share a meal.

The five days

🧹

Day 1 · Dhanteras

Homes are cleaned. Families buy something new for good fortune.

🪔

Day 2 · Choti Diwali

Marks the defeat of Narakasura. Diyas are lit at dusk.

✨

Day 3 · Lakshmi Puja

The main night, with lamps, prayers, sweets and fireworks.

⛰️

Day 4 · Govardhan Puja

Remembers Krishna lifting Govardhan hill. New Year in some regions.

👫

Day 5 · Bhai Dooj

Sisters pray for their brothers, who give gifts in return.

Why we celebrate

🏹

Rama returns

People lit lamps to welcome Rama, Sita and Lakshmana home to Ayodhya.

🔱

Kali Puja

In Bengal, Odisha and Assam the night honours goddess Kali.

☸️

Jain Diwali

Jains remember Lord Mahavira attaining moksha.

🕊️

Bandi Chhor Divas

Sikhs celebrate Guru Hargobind’s release from Gwalior fort.

Diyas & Rangoli

Diyas make homes bright and rangoli adds colour. Try the rangoli below.

Decorate your home

🪔

Light diyas

Place them near doors, windows and balconies. Soak clay diyas first so they burn longer.

🌼

Marigold torans

Strings of marigold and mango leaves on the door welcome guests.

🏮

Lanterns and lights

Hang akash kandils and fairy lights on balconies.

🪷

Floating flowers

Petals and a floating candle in a bowl make a quick centrepiece.

Colour your own rangoli

Pick a colour, then tap the petals.

Crackers & Fun

Fireworks make Diwali exciting, but enjoy them with care. Launch some in the sky below.

Enjoy responsibly

🎆

Watch from a distance

Enjoy the colours from a safe spot with family.

🧯

Safety first

Keep water or sand nearby and never light crackers indoors.

👕

Wear fitted cotton

Avoid loose dupattas, scarves and synthetic fabric.

🌍

Be kind to nature

Choose fewer, quieter, eco-friendly crackers, and keep pets calm.

Stay Safe

A happy Diwali is a safe Diwali.

Simple rules

🌳

Open spaces only

Light crackers outdoors, away from vehicles and dry grass.

💧

Water nearby

Keep a bucket of water or sand ready.

👶

Adult supervision

Never let small children handle crackers alone.

🚫

Don’t relight

Leave a dud cracker alone and pour water on it.

🕯️

Careful with diyas

Keep lamps away from curtains and never leave them unattended.

🩹

Burns

Cool minor burns under running water for 10 minutes. Get medical help for serious ones.

📜

Follow local rules

Many cities restrict crackers or allow only green ones.
